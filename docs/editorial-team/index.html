Team

Editorial team

Two writers, one rubric, and a rule that no vendor gets a say in what we publish.

AI Tool Scout is written by two people who use these tools in their own work every week. There is no newsroom behind us, no syndication partner, and no model rewriting our drafts. Every article on this site carries one of the two names below, and that name is accountable for it.

SW

Sarah Whitfield

Contributing editor. Sarah writes about writing tools, prompting, research workflows and the practical side of getting useful output from a language model. She spent eight years in content operations before covering this space, which is where the habit of checking a vendor's pricing page and terms of service before writing anything came from. She has published 16 articles on AI Tool Scout, including our comparisons of writing assistants and our prompt framework.

ML

Marcus Lindqvist

Editor and analyst. Marcus covers model releases, agents, developer tooling, licensing and the cost of running AI systems. His background is in software engineering and technical evaluation, which is why the numbers in his articles come from provider documentation, published benchmarks and reproducible tests rather than from a launch post. He has published 14 articles on AI Tool Scout, including our agent and infrastructure pieces.

How we work

  1. Primary sources first. Pricing, limits, data policies and commercial rights come from the provider's own documentation, not from its marketing page.
  2. One rubric for every tool. Comparisons score the same criteria: output quality, instruction following, reliability, workflow fit, and price against value.
  3. Facts and opinion are separated. A documented fact is sourced. A judgment is marked as a judgment. A score is an editorial rating, explained inside the article.
  4. Re-check on major releases. These products change monthly. When something significant ships, we re-check the affected claims and date the update.
  5. No paid coverage. We do not accept payment for a review, a score or a placement — see the about page for how the site is funded.
Corrections

If you spot an error, an outdated price, or a claim you think we got wrong, tell us. We publish corrections openly at the bottom of the affected article rather than editing silently, and we credit the change.
